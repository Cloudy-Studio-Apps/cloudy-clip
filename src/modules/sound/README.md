# sound

The Sound page, a tab between Edit and Export, and the mix of a composition: how each audio track and the master sound, with two mixes to switch between. The page has a transport row, the audio-only timeline over a panel (Clip, Channel, Loudness and Deliver; it opens on Channel) and a small preview on the left, and the mixer on the right with Master pinned at its edge (layout B, `docs/prototype/designs/sound-page.html`). The values live in `project` (`Track.mix`, `Composition.mix`), since core modules read them; this module owns the page, the commands and the tools.

- **Tier:** feature
- **Requires:** `export` (the Audio format card edits its form), `project`, `timeline` (the panel, `setTrackMuted`, `splitAt`, Snap), `preview` (the stage, play, the timecode, live mix, solo, monitor, meters, loudness, `dynamicsTransfer`), `inspector` (`ClipTabSections` in the Clip tab; `FieldRow` in the Channel tab's Ducking card), `audio` (`EqEditor`, `SoundRow`), `keyframe` (`KeyDiamond`, `keyframeRef`'s key selection, `setKey`, `moveKeys`, `setKeyValues`, `removeKeys`), `settings` (the Sound presets section), `media` (noise reduction's rendered files, `getProbe`; `prepareFile` to read a take before `media.import` places it), `workspace` (the project's folder for a take, `beforeLeave`)
- **Contributes:** track-header controls `sound.solo` (S, order 10) and `sound.arm` (R, order 20) (`timelineTrackControls`, scope `sound`); track-header control `sound.lineView` (L, order 30, scope `sound`: the track's volume line in front); timeline tools (`timelineTools`, scope `sound`) `sound.drawVolume` (the pen, right after Split: Draw volume), `sound.automation` ("Automation: Touch", at the end: the marked track's mode, or Master's) and `sound.sheetNote` ("3 tracks, 1:32", at the end); `clipActions` badge `sound.clipLine` and timeline overlay `sound.clipLineKeys` (both scopes `["all", "sound"]`: the clip volume line, below); timeline overlay `sound.trackLine` (scopes `["all", "sound"]`: the track volume line and Draw volume, below); timeline overlay `sound.masterLine` (scope `sound`: the master's line lane, below); timeline overlay `sound.recordingTake` (`timelineOverlays`, scope `sound`: the take growing on its lane, or on a dashed lane of its own under the last track while it goes onto a new Voice track (under the ruler and the marks row, `timeline`'s `rowsAbove(scope)`, when there is no track yet), `ui/lanes/RecordingTake.tsx`); page `sound.page` (`SOUND_PAGE`; `appPages`, icon `AudioLines`, `keepsPlayback: true`, `timelineScope: "sound"`: playback goes on and Space, S and Delete work here, and the timeline edits sound only); section `sound.presets` (`settingsSections`, "Sound presets", `ui/settings/PresetsSection.tsx`); loudness matcher `sound.match` (`export`'s `loudnessMatchers`: Match target on export, below); snap target `sound.beats` (`timelineSnapTargets`: the beats found, below) and timeline overlay `sound.beatTicks` (scopes `["all", "sound"]`, `ui/lanes/BeatTicks.tsx`)
- **Session tools on the page:** markers (`marker`: flags, snapping and the M keys reach the Sound timeline), the loop (Loop in the transport, handles in the marks row under the ruler, which `timeline` draws on the sound scope; markers' flags sit there too) and audio scrub (dragging on the ruler plays slices of sound through the mix), and recording a voice onto a track (below).
- **Points it owns:** —
- **API** (`SoundApi`, per kernel): `view` (`SoundViewState`: `selected` (a track id, a group id, `"master"` or null), `solo`, `expanded`, `filter` (the group whose strips the mixer shows, or null for all), `monitor`, `armed` (the track a take goes onto); never in history), `select(target | null)`, `setExpanded(expanded)`, `setFilter(busId | null)`, `arm(trackId | null)` (refused while a take is under way), `recorder` (`Recorder`: `state` (`status`: `idle`, `starting`, `recording`, `stopping`; `trackId`, null during a take onto a new Voice track until it's placed; `at`), `start(trackId | null)` (null: onto a new Voice track), `stop({ pause? })` (returns the stop under way, if any), `arm(trackId | null)`, `level()` and `length()`, read per frame), `presets` (the user's own, `UserPresets`), `analyzer` (whole-mix analyses: `state`, `start`, `cancel`, `settled`), `matcher` (Match target: `start`, `cancel`, `settled`, and `match` for an export), `beats` (`BeatFinder`: `state` (`BeatsState`: `files`, beats by file path in file microseconds; `running`, `{ progress }` or null; `failed`), `find(paths)`, `cancel()`, `settled()`). The commands are exported from `index.ts`.

  - `sound.effects.commands.ts`: the effect-chain commands below, on an audio track, a group (its `BusId`) or `"master"`. Every one reads the chain, edits a copy and writes it in one step through `sound.mix.write.ts` (`writeTrack`, `writeComposition`, `targetComposition`, shared with the mix commands), so an empty chain stores nothing. `setEffect` merges the patch into the effect and parses it with the kind's schema (`effectSchemas` in `project`), so out-of-range values and unknown fields are refused. `logic/effects.ts`: `defaultSpec(kind)` (typed by kind), `chainWhere(target, compositionId)` (the `{ target, compositionId? }` input every chain command from the UI takes), `closesLoop(tracks, owner, listensTo, buses)` (walks the listens-to graph, where a group also depends on the tracks sent to it; self counts) and `listensOf(track)`. `logic/effects.ts` also has `EFFECT_NAMES` (full and short), `addableKinds(chain, target, group)` (not there yet; no duck on master; no noise reduction on a group), `chainOf(state, target, compositionId)` (the stored array, one shared empty one, so selectors are stable) `placesUnheardEffects(state, trackId)` (a placed composition with effects on its master, a group or a track). `logic/denoise.ts`: `denoiseRequests(needs, hasSound)` (the renderings `project`'s `denoiseNeeds` calls for, files with sound only: the forward rendering for every need, which export mixes from, then the reversed copy for reversed clips, which the preview plays). `logic/chain.ts`: `chainSlot` (where a dragged row lands). `logic/presets.ts`: `BUILT_IN_PRESETS` (Clear voice, Podcast, Phone, Radio, Warm) as plain data.
  - `sound.denoise.commands.ts`: `sound.measureFloor` reads the noise floor of a second (or `duration`) of a track at `at` (default the actor's playhead) through `sound.service.ts` (`measureNoiseFloor`, the native `sound_measure_floor` in `src-tauri/src/sound/`: the overall noise floor `astats` reads over a stretch of the original, rounded, within what `afftdn` accepts, -80 to -20 dBFS) and sets the floor of the track's noise reduction with `sound.setEffect`, one undo step. Noise reduction itself is rendered per file and settings, which the preview plays and the export mixes from; `project`'s `heardDenoise` and `denoiseNeeds` say which clips get it: the top-level track's own, otherwise the master's (per file, for the tracks without their own; not a bus effect). It always applies first, before clip volume and the rest of the chain, wherever it sits in the chain. `sound.denoise.follow.ts` (`followDenoiseNeeds`, from `setup`): 800 ms after the document or the probes last change, every file `denoiseNeeds` lists in any composition is wanted (`media.wantDenoised`) and only those, so a run of nudges renders the last setting and queued ones left behind are dropped.
  - **Whole-mix analysis, native side** (stage 5; the command, store and UI come next): `src-tauri/src/sound/analyze.rs` (what is measured and how FFmpeg's lines are read) and `analyses.rs` (the running ones), three Tauri commands. `sound_analyze_begin(spec, peakLimit, onProgress)` takes a `SoundOnlySpec` (`duration`, `sound`, `mix`: an export spec's sound, built as a Mix export's, never its stems), the peak limit in dBTP and a `Channel<number>` that gets 0 to 1 each hundredth, and returns a job id; a mix with no sound is refused (`invalid_input`). `sound_analyze_finish(jobId)` returns `Analysis` `{ integrated, range, truePeak, problems }`; `sound_analyze_cancel(jobId)` stops it (finish then fails with `Cancelled`). It renders the export's own mix graph (`export`'s `measure_arguments`, the same builder) without playing it, through `asetnsamples` (100 ms frames), `ebur128=peak=true:metadata=1`, `astats` per frame and the same at 192 kHz, to `-f null`, in 32-bit float; the job, its cancel and its failure message are an export job's (`Job::measure`), kept in `analyses.rs`'s own registry (an export command cannot reach one). FFmpeg's `loudnorm` is never used. `integrated` (LUFS) is null when nothing is above `ebur128`'s −70 LUFS gate, a silent mix included ("nothing to measure", never −inf), and `range` (LU) is then 0; `truePeak` (dBTP) is null for digital silence. `problems`, each `{ at, duration, kind, level }` in microseconds: `"peak"` is a stretch of frames whose true peak (the peak at four times the rate, as BS.1770 defines it) is over the limit, adjacent frames merged and `level` the highest; `"clipped"` is a stretch with a sample peak over 0 dBFS (by more than 0.0001 dB, as `astats` prints 6 decimals; a full-scale 0 dBFS peak is not a clip), which a file of whole numbers would cut off (so also within a peak stretch). Sorted by `at`. The figures are the mix at 48 kHz, before an export's rate and dither.
  - **Find beats** (stage 6): `src-tauri/src/sound/beats.rs` decodes a file's first sound stream to mono floats at 11025 Hz (a scratch file noted like an export's partials) as an export `Job` (its progress from `-progress`, its cancel), then finds onsets (spectral flux, 512-sample frames every 64 samples, 5.8 ms, the FFT's twiddles computed once), and per 10 s window, half a window apart (each keeps the beats nearer its middle than another's), the tempo (autocorrelation of the onsets between 60 and 200 BPM, leaning to 120; under 0.3 of the window's energy it has no tempo: speech, a held tone and silence have no beats, and that is no error) and the beats (the best chain of onsets one period apart, Ellis's dynamic programming, so strays off the beat are never picked; after a gap a chain starts again, so a break keeps the beats on both sides; a beat with no onset under it is dropped). A song that changes tempo has beats in each part; the window across the change can miss a beat or two. Progress is decoding (80 %), then the spectra and the windows. The thresholds (0.3, the 120 BPM lean, an onset of 1 standard deviation) were tuned on synthetic sound only (clicks, a generated kick pattern, irregular bursts); the lean may pick half or double tempo on songs far from 120 BPM. A sound stream that starts late adds its delay. Results are cached in Rust by path, size and modified time for the app's lifetime. Tauri commands `sound_beats(path, jobId, onProgress) -> { beats }` and `sound_beats_cancel(jobId)`, called through `sound.service.ts` (`findFileBeats`, `cancelFileBeats`). `sound.beats.ts` (`createBeatFinder`) runs the files one after another outside the bus, keeps each file's beats in `sound.beats.store.ts` (per kernel, never in the project or history) and tells a file's failure in a toast (the others go on). `logic/beats.ts`: `clipBeats(clip, beats)` puts a file's beats on the timeline under a clip (trims, speed, ramp, reverse, through `timeOfContent`; only those it plays; a held frame none) and `compositionBeats(state, compositionId, files)` does it for every clip on an audio track; `rememberedBeats()` is that, answering again while the document, composition and beats are the same objects (the snap target uses it, so a drag doesn't scan per move). `sound.beats.mcp.ts`: the beat tools. Snapping (`sound.beats`) and the ticks (`BeatTicks`: a 1 px `--on` tick in the lower half of the ruler row `useFlagRow()` gives, only near the viewport, no pointer) both read it; nothing is placed by itself. Find beats in the transport (`ui/transport/QuickTools.tsx`) runs `sound.findBeats` on the timeline's selected clips, or on every audio clip of the open composition when none is selected; while it runs it reads "Beats 40%" and a click is `sound.cancelBeats`. Only clips of the composition itself count, not those inside a nested composition.
  - `sound.presets.commands.ts`: `sound.savePreset`, `sound.renamePreset`, `sound.removePreset` on the user's own presets. `sound.presets.store.ts`: `createUserPresets()`, `core`'s `createSavedStore` under `localStorage` key `cloudy-clip.sound-presets` (each preset parsed on its own, its effects with their kind's schema from `project` without the id; one that doesn't parse is dropped, never the whole list). Never in a project: applying one writes plain values. A saved duck listens to no track (tracks belong to one project). No MCP tools: agents list the user's presets (`sound_list_presets`, `own: true`) and apply them, never change them.
  - `sound.monitor.commands.ts`: `sound.setSolo`, `sound.setMonitor` and `sound.clearListening`. They write `view` and push the result to `preview.sound` (`setSolo`, `setMonitor`). Not undoable and no MCP tools: they act on the user's ears, like `settings.open`.
  - `sound.leave.ts`: `followSoundPageLeave(pages, onLeave)`. The module runs `sound.clearListening` each time the user leaves the Sound page: solo, Mono and Dim show nowhere else, so Edit never plays soloed or dimmed without saying so. The speaker volume stays, like a knob on the desk.
  - **Recording** (Linux; see `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-03-microphone-check.md`). Where it is offered is one constant, `RECORDING_CHECKED` in `logic/record.ts` (`linux: true`, `windows: false` until the owner's WebView2 check passes, `macos: false` until the bundle has `NSMicrophoneUsageDescription`, `android: false`); elsewhere Record and the arm buttons are absent (`canRecordHere`, in `sound.record.capture.ts`). R on a track's header (`ui/lanes/ArmButton.tsx`, a `LetterToggle` from `@/ui`) arms an audio track that isn't muted (`canArm`; a muted track isn't offered; groups, when they come, won't be either); `recorder.arm` refuses while a take is under way. **Record records onto the armed track, or onto a new Voice track when none is armed.** Record in the transport (`ui/transport/RecordButton.tsx`, enabled whenever a composition is open) runs `recorder.start` with the armed track when it can take the take (an audio track that isn't muted, in the open composition), otherwise with null: the take then goes onto a new audio track at the bottom of the open composition, named `Voice 1`, `Voice 2`… (`VOICE_TRACK` in `logic/record.ts`, numbered by `timeline`'s `nextTrackName`), made by `sound.placeRecording` in the take's own undo step, so one undo removes the take and its track together, and a take with nothing kept, or that fails, leaves no track behind. Until then `RecordingTake` draws it on a dashed lane under the last track, named as the track will be (the mixer has no strip for it yet, so no input meter); once placed, the new track is armed, so the next take goes there too. Record is an icon, its mark (`ui/transport/RecordDot.tsx`, a small square) in `--mark`. While the microphone opens, and while the take is written and placed, it's disabled with the mark pulsing, and its tooltip says "Opening the microphone" or "Saving the take"; while it records it's a Stop filled `--mark` (the mark in `--mark-foreground`, pulsing), followed by how long the take has run, `mm:ss` (`ui/transport/RecordingClock.tsx`: read from `recorder.length()` each frame, written through the DOM only when the second changes). `recorder.start`: `sound.record.capture.ts` (`openCapture`) asks for the microphone with echo cancellation, noise suppression and gain control off, and runs it through its own `AudioContext` into `sound.record.worklet.ts` (bundled with `?worker&url` and loaded with `audioWorklet.addModule`, as the preview's loudness and dynamics worklets are), which keeps nothing until `keep()` (sent right after playback starts, so the take starts with the next block) and posts the first channel in batches of 512 samples; stopping flushes the last partial batch before the microphone closes. The take is recorded at that context's rate. **Where takes go:** `Recordings/` in the project's folder; for a project with no file yet, `Recordings/` in the workspace's `folder()` (Documents/Cloudy Clip), where that project will be saved, so the take ends up beside it, never in a cache. `sound.service.ts`: `beginTake` (`sound_record_begin`: `Recording <date> <time>.wav`, ` 2`, ` 3`… when taken, never overwritten; the project's folder must exist, or at least its parent; a take a reloaded page left open is closed first) and `writeTake` (`sound_record_write`: 16-bit samples as a raw body, a quarter of a second at a time, at most 1 MB a chunk; the native side rewrites the WAV header after every chunk, so the file is whole even if the app stops; `done` closes it, and a take with nothing in it is deleted). Then `preview.play`, and the take starts at the playhead where playback started (`at`). It runs on past the composition's end. While it records the loop is off (playback runs on through its end; it's set again after the take, if its composition is still open) and audio scrub is silenced (`preview.holdScrub`), so the open microphone never hears a slice. While it records, the armed strip's meters show the microphone (`Meter` target `"input"`, from `recorder.level()` through the page's meter loop) and `RecordingTake` draws the take on its lane each frame through `style` until it's placed; the document doesn't change until then. Stop (the button, a pause, playing again after the end, any jump of the playhead by more than a frame from where the time passed puts it (a ruler click or drag, Home, a marker, a seek from anywhere: the follower watches the user's clock, so no caller checks), leaving the Sound page, opening another composition: `sound.record.follow.ts`; what was recorded up to then is placed; the project about to be left, through the workspace's `beforeLeave`) pauses, flushes and closes the file, reads it outside the bus (`media.prepareFile`) and runs `sound.placeRecording` as its own undoable step. `stop()` during a stop returns the one under way, so window close waits for the placement before saving. `workspace.new` and `workspace.open` refuse with `workspace.recording` while a take is under way. What can't be placed, or a write that failed (a full disk), is told with the file's path; a take is never lost silently. `sound.record.ts` (`createRecorder`), `logic/record.ts` (`toPcm16`, `joinChunks`, `takeStem`, `folderOf`, `startFailure`, `platformOf`, `formatElapsed`, `VOICE_TRACK`). Native side: `src-tauri/src/sound/record.rs` (`Takes`), `microphone.rs` (`permission_request`, set on the builder in `lib.rs`: the microphone is allowed for the app's own pages only, the dev server's `http://localhost:1420` in debug builds only; WebKitGTK denies it otherwise, with no prompt). No MCP tool: the microphone is the user's.
  - **Clip volume line** (`ui/lanes/ClipVolumeLine.tsx`, `ui/lanes/clipline.gesture.ts`, `ui/lanes/ClipLineKeys.tsx`, `logic/clipline.ts`): over each clip of an audio track, the clip's `sound.volumeDb` as a line in `--on` (flat at the static level when unkeyed) with `keyframe`'s `KeyDiamond` for each key on the clip; a view of the keys that exist, with no data of its own. An unkeyed line shows only on a selected or hovered clip; a keyed one always. It sits in a box from 24px under the top (below the fade handles and keyframe's toggle) to the bottom edge, and an unkeyed line takes no pointer until it shows. A segment is drawn with extra points where it bends: across a speed ramp or an ease, and where it crosses a taper breakpoint (`TAPER_DBS`), so the drawn line matches what is heard, so handles, the gain badge and keyframe's K toggle stay reachable. Points are placed by `contentTime` / `timeOfContent`, so they sit where the sound is on clips with speed and on split clips; the level is the fader's taper (`faderPosition` / `faderDb`) over `VOLUME_DB_MIN`..`VOLUME_DB_MAX`. Dragging a point writes the line (`polylineOf`, `movedLine`) and the diamond with `style` / attributes only; the release is one `keyframe.move` (time changed, with the new value) or `keyframe.setValues` (level only); Escape or a refused command puts it back. A double-click on the line is `keyframe.set` at that time (the line's own value there); a press on a point selects it (the key selection of `keyframe`, `focused`) and the clip, and Delete / Backspace is `keyframe.remove`. That key path is one capture listener (`ClipLineKeys`, an overlay with no drawing, which also deletes a selected track-line point) that stops the event before the timeline's Delete, so the clip stays; while `keyframe`'s panel is open it handles the same selection itself and the overlay steps aside. A press elsewhere lets go of the selection.
  - **Track volume line** (`ui/lanes/TrackVolumeLine.tsx`, `ui/lanes/TrackLineLane.tsx`, `logic/trackline.ts`): over each audio lane of the open composition, the track's `volumeLine` in the same box and taper as a clip's line (`lineXs` in `logic/clipline.ts`, shared: keys, steps, taper crossings, samples where it bends). An overlay at `z-[11]`, over the clips and under the headers; each lane is measured from the timeline's `[data-track-id]` lane. Drawn only for the viewport and one either side (`from`..`to`, again when the scroll reaches another viewport), so an hour-long line costs a screen of it. Behind the clips' lines (the default, and always on the Edit page) it's dashed and faint and takes no pointer; **in front** (`view.lineView`, L on the track's header, `ui/lanes/LineViewButton.tsx`; it takes effect on the Sound timeline only, so the Edit page draws every line as without it) it's solid, a press on a diamond selects it (`view.linePoint`, one selected point across clip and track lines: pressing a clip line's point clears it, and it clears the key selection) and drags it (`clipline.gesture.ts`'s `startLineGesture`; one `sound.moveLineKeys` on release, Escape puts it back), Delete / Backspace on a selected point is `sound.removeLineKeys` through `ClipLineKeys`'s one capture listener (Ruling 2), a double-click on the line adds a point at its level there (`sound.setLineKey`), it shows flat at 0 dB with no keys, and the clips' lines of that track show at 30 % and take no pointer. With Draw volume each lane gets `ui/lanes/TrackLineStroke.tsx`, the surface that takes strokes. A lane takes a `target` (a track id, or `"master"`) and its `line`; `ui/lanes/lane.page.hook.ts`'s `useLanePage` is the viewport logic (root, `from`..`to`, zoom, header width, a counter for re-measuring) that `TrackVolumeLine` and `MasterLine` share. **The master's line** (`ui/lanes/MasterLine.tsx` and `MasterBand.tsx`, a `timelineOverlays` contribution `sound.masterLine`, Sound page only): a row under the last track, eight pixels below its lane, with a header that says Master and a lane (`data-master-lane`) holding the master's `volumeLine` through the same `TrackLineLane`, always in front: drag a point, double-click the line to add one, Delete removes the selected point (`view.linePoint` holds `"master"` as its `trackId`; `ClipLineKeys` sends `sound.removeLineKeys { target: "master" }`), and Draw volume takes strokes (`sound.writePass { target: "master" }`). It is the line Touch and Latch write on the master strip, whose fader is a trim on it. Hidden while the composition has no track.
  - **Draw volume** (`view.tool`, `"select"` or `"draw"`: Draw volume in the transport and the pen in the timeline's tools are the same tool, a `view` write, never a command): on the Sound page every audio lane takes strokes (crosshair, `TrackLineStroke`). A stroke (`ui/lanes/trackline.gesture.ts`, `startStroke`; drawn in `--mark` through the DOM; Escape drops it) is sent on release (`ui/lanes/trackline.stroke.ts`, `sendStroke`), its points read on the fader's taper and thinned to within 0.5 dB (`logic/automation.ts` `thinLevels`, through `project`'s `simplify`): with the track's line in front, one `sound.writePass` over the stroke's span; otherwise one `sound.writeClipPass` on the clip the stroke starts over, kept inside it and mapped on its line's box (`data-clipline-area`); over no clip, nothing. Both leave the line outside the stroke as it played: `project`'s `writeSpan` replaces the keys in the span with the stroke's points, straight between them, and holds the old level 1 µs outside each edge (the clip's static `volumeDb` when it has no keys); the clip's keys are written in content time, in one `keyframe.writeSpan`.
  - **Automation** (`sound.automation.ts`, `createAutomation`; `ui/lanes/automation.hook.ts`, `useAutomationFader`; `logic/automation.ts`): each track, and the master of the open composition (`"master"`, a `LineTarget`), has a mode in `view.automation` (Read by default; view state, never in history): the strip's button (`ui/mixer/ReadButton.tsx`, named Read, Touch or Latch, on in `--mark` when not Read) and "Automation: …" in the timeline's tools (`ui/lanes/AutomationNote.tsx`, the marked track's) go to the next on a click. Read plays the line. In Touch or Latch, pressing the fader while playing starts a **pass** (a trim write): the fader's moves are heard at once (`previewMix` with the fader's level, the offset on the line, over the line as it is) and change no volume. A pass keeps the track's line as it was when it began (`snapshot`) and writes relative to it each frame (`playback.onTime`), `{at, db}`: first the line's own level (so the line before the pass stays), then the line raised or lowered by how far the fader is from the track's volume. A seek or the loop going round (the playhead back, or more than half a second ahead) starts another **lap** of the same pass, never a send, so a looped Latch writes the same change each lap instead of adding it up. In Touch, the release returns to the line: its own level at that moment and, where the line bends there, its levels every 100 ms up to the next key (`returnToLine`), so the line after the release keeps its level and curve; the saved mix plays and nothing is written until the next press, which starts another lap of the same pass (from the line's own level), so every hold of one play is one pass and one undo step. In Latch it keeps writing at the last level until playback stops. A pass ends, and is sent once as one `sound.writePasses` (one undo step), when playback stops (Touch too), when the mode changes, on leaving the Sound page or the composition (`sound.leave.ts`: `followSoundPageLeave`, `followCompositionSwitch`, the same helpers the recorder follows), and before the project is left (`workspace.beforeLeave`). Its laps are joined (`passSpans`): a later lap replaces an earlier one where they overlap, overlapping laps make one span, each span is thinned to within 0.5 dB, and laps that don't touch (a seek ahead) stay separate spans, so the keys between them are left alone, ids and eases included. A pass removes itself before it's sent, so two of these at once send it once. A pass in which the fader never left its place sends nothing. Then the saved mix plays again. **A change to the track's line from anywhere else during a pass ends it and sends nothing** (an undo or redo, `sound.switchMix`, an agent's edit: the writer follows the project store and drops a pass whose track's `volumeLine` is no longer its `snapshot`; the pass's own send can't trip it, since a pass is removed before it sends). What it recorded was written against a line that is gone, so sending it would write over the new one; the safe choice is to keep the new line and let the user play the pass again. If playback stops under the hold, the fader is still heard as it moves, and writes a new pass if playback starts again; let go, the saved mix plays. A drag while paused, or in Read, sets the track's volume as before. The module's teardown drops passes without sending (leaving the project has sent them already). The master works the same way on its own volume line (`MasterMix.volumeLine`), with the master's volume as the offset: a pass on it remembers the composition open when it began (`AutomationPass.compositionId`) and sends `sound.writePasses { target: "master", compositionId }`; `logic/automation.ts`'s `lineFader(state, target, compositionId)` reads a track's or the master's line and volume for both. - **Invariants:** a mix equal to the default is never stored. Only audio tracks have a mix. Switching A and B swaps the live values (every audio track's mix, the groups and the master) with `otherMix`; groups are shared structure (the same ids, names and order in both mixes: `sound.addBus` adds to both, `sound.setBus` writes the name to both), and only volume, mute and effects swap; the first time, B starts as a copy of A, and tracks deleted since are left out. A switch is one undo step and swaps the effect chains with the volumes. A chain holds one effect of each kind, on a track or a group; a duck (on a track or a group) may listen to tracks and groups but never to itself or into a loop (a group depends on the tracks sent to it), the master cannot duck (`sound.duck_on_master`, in every command that writes a chain), and a deleted track leaves every `listensTo` in the step that deletes it (`project`'s `settle`). `sound.resetTrackMix` keeps the effects and the volume line. A track's volume line (`TrackMix.volumeLine`, keys on timeline time), and the master's (`MasterMix.volumeLine`), are edited only through `sound.setLineKey`, `sound.moveLineKeys`, `sound.removeLineKeys`, `sound.writePass` and `sound.writePasses`, which share `project`'s key helpers and ease schema (`keyEase`) with clip keys (`moveLineKeys` merges points within half a frame, `writePass` only exact times, and holds the old level just outside its span); a line with no keys is dropped. Each line command takes `target` (a track id or `"master"`, with `compositionId` for another composition's master; `lineOwner` in `sound.line.commands.ts` finds the line, half a frame of its composition and how to write it, so tracks and the master share every command). The line is heard on the track's bus fader (the master's on the master fader) with the fader's volume as an offset on top, in the preview and the export alike (`preview`'s and `export`'s READMEs). Solo and the monitor live in this module's `view`, per kernel, and never reach the document, history or an export. Solo, Mono and Dim last only while the Sound page is open.

## Delivery (stage 5, in the document)

`logic/standards.ts` holds the standards as data (`STANDARDS`), each figure with the page it comes from (read 9 Nov 2026) and `published: false` where the owner publishes none: YouTube -14 LUFS and -1 dBTP (convention; no figure published), Spotify -14 and -1 (support.spotify.com loudness normalization), Apple Podcasts -16 and -1 (podcasters.apple.com audio requirements), TikTok -14 and -1 (convention; no figure published), EBU R128 -23 and -1 (R 128 v5.0, tech.ebu.ch), ATSC A/85 -24 and -2 (A/85:2026-07 Annex M), Custom (editable; starts at -14 and -1). The delivery types (analyses, Match target, the Deliver view) are in `sound.delivery.types.ts`. `sound.analysis.store.ts` holds the analyses per composition (view state): the result with the project state it measured, so any later edit of the project (of any kind, audio or not) or undo makes it stale (`isStale`), and so does Match target's change (it then analyzes where the mix landed, below); `sound.analysis.ts` (`createAnalyzer`, `SoundApi.analyzer`) runs them outside the bus (its 30 s timeout is short for a long mix), reports progress and can cancel; disposing the module cancels running jobs. `export`'s `soundOnlySpec` builds the spec with the Mix export's own `mixdown`. `loudnorm` is never used.

## Match target (stage 5)

Match target brings a composition's mix to its delivery target (`logic/match.ts`, pure; `sound.match.ts`, `createMatcher`, `SoundApi.matcher`; `sound.measure.ts`, `measureOnce`, one analysis on the side that is stored nowhere). It writes the project once:

1. **Measure:** the stored analysis when it is fresh; otherwise it analyzes (`analyzer`, stored as any analysis).
2. **First step** (`trimChange`): Master's input trim (`MasterMix.trimDb`, before the fader, its line, pan and effects, after the stems are taken: the stage an export's correction sits on, so here and on export mean the same) moves by target - integrated, within -24..24; the fader stays the user's. The limiter (`limiterPlan`): a working Limiter on Master is the user's and stays as it is; one that is off is switched on (its release kept) and moved to the end of the chain when it isn't last; with none, a new one at the end (release 50 ms), both at the peak limit: where the correction's limiter sits.
3. **Measure again on the side:** the analysis spec with the first step and that limiter as a correction (`export`'s `withCorrection`, `MasterMixSpec.correction`: the same override an export uses); the project isn't touched.
4. **Second step** (`secondStep`): a limiter lowers loudness, more the harder it works, so the level still missing is divided by how much of each dB the limiter let through over the first step (the loudness lost over the dB its ceiling was passed by; at least a quarter) and capped at `CORRECTION_CAP_DB` (3 dB): past it more level would only drive the limiter, so the mix lands short instead. A limiter (sample peaks) lets a true peak over its ceiling on bright sound; when the mix went over the peak limit and the limiter is matching's own, its ceiling comes down by the overshoot and a tenth (`ceilingDb`).
5. **One change:** `sound.matchTarget` with `correctionDb` and `ceilingDb`, run as whoever asked (`MatchBy`), one undo step; then an analysis of where the mix landed (`MatchOutcome.landed`, the fresh stored result the Deliver tab shows).

Starting Match cancels an analysis still running for an older state of the project (it does not wait for it). It keeps the project the spec was built from (`origin`): the stored analysis counts only if it measured `origin` (an analysis it starts is labelled with `origin`, `analyzer.start`'s `measured`), and if the project is anything else at any step, before the write too, it stops and changes nothing ("The project changed while matching"). Progress is in the analyses' store (`matching[id]`: `step` `measuring`, `correcting` or `landing`, and `progress`), the last outcome in `matched`, a failure in `matchFailed` (and a toast). `sound.cancelAnalysis` stops it.

**On export** (`export.enqueue`'s `matchTarget`, the Export page's "Match the delivery target", the Stems card's "Match target on export"): `matcher.match`, contributed to `export`'s `loudnessMatchers`, measures the job's own mix, works out the same two steps and returns a `MasterCorrection`, which the runner adds to that job's spec only: the same trim range, so it lands where Match target here would. Stems are taken before Master and don't get it.

Checked by hand (5 s generated mixes through the real mix graph and FFmpeg, target -14 LUFS, limit -1 dBTP): a mix at -32.3 LUFS / -21.8 dBTP lands at -14.0 / -3.5 here (trim +18.3) and on export; a peaky one at -33.2 / -17.2 lands at -14.3 / -1.2 both ways (trim +22.2, ceiling down to -1.3). `clamped` only past the trim's ±24 dB.

**Notes on what the figures mean and when they hold:**

- Figures are measured on the 48 kHz float mix, before the codec: MP3, AAC and Opus can add a few tenths of a dB of true peak (which is what the -1 and -2 dBTP limits leave room for).
- Staleness is the whole project's identity: any project edit (a title, a marker, another composition included) marks an analysis stale and aborts a running Match target with "The project changed while matching". That is safe, if costly on a long mix.
- Opening another project (`ctx.replace`), or removing the composition, cancels its running analyses and Match targets (`sound.analysis.follow.ts`, `followCompositionsGone`).
- Match target says in its outcome when it switched a limiter on or moved it to the end of Master's chain (`limiterSwitched`, `limiterMoved`; the Deliver tab says so). `peakOver` (`{ overDb, userLimiter }`, also shown in red on the Match row) is set when the last measured true peak, the analysis of where the mix landed, is still over the peak limit: `userLimiter` when the user's own limiter has its ceiling above the limit; otherwise peaks between samples got past a limiter at or under it. It is not measured a third time.
