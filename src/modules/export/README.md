# export

Turns a composition into a file. The Export page, the last tab of the top bar, sets what to make; exports wait in a queue and run one at a time while you keep editing.

The settings sections fold one by one, and all open again when you come back to the page.

- **Tier:** feature
- **Requires:** `project`, `media`, `preview`, `workspace` (the default file name)
- **Contributes:** page `export.page` (`appPages`, group `PAGE_GROUP_DELIVER`, icon `Upload`)
- **Points it owns:** `loudnessMatchers` (`export.points.ts`): what measures a mix and works out the `MasterCorrection` that brings it to its delivery target (`LoudnessMatcher.match`); `sound` contributes the one. Without one, "Match the delivery target" isn't offered and `enqueue` refuses `matchTarget`.
- **API** (`useModule(exportRef)` / `ctx.require(exportRef)`): `queue`, `form`, `capabilities`, `runner`, `canMatchTarget()` (a matcher is there). `index.ts` also exports the format, sample rate and bit depth lists (`EXPORT_FORMATS`, `EXPORT_SAMPLE_RATES`, `EXPORT_BIT_DEPTHS`) and `fileRate`, `hasBitDepth`, which `sound`'s Audio format card uses, and `stemsOf`, which its Stems card uses
- **Files:**
  - Speed: frames come from the preview engine, which follows `sourceTimeAt`. Sound carries `speed`, `keepPitch` and `reversed` to `src-tauri/src/export/recipe.rs`, which reads `duration * speed` of the file, and `soundfilters.rs` adds `areverse`, then `atempo` (or `asetrate` when `keepPitch` is false) before the clip's own settings. Reversed clips get a full-quality copy before rendering (`media.prepareReversed`). A ramped clip's sound starts at `sourceTimeAt(clip, start)` and carries `tempoSteps` instead of `speed`: one for each stretch where the rate holds (before the first key, after the last, on a Hold or between equal keys) and one per 250 ms of timeline time where it changes (rounded, the last one taking what is left, none shorter than 50 ms unless the segment is, so their lengths add up to the segment's), each with its `from` in the file and the `speed` that plays its part of the file in its time. `soundfilters.rs` splits the input (`asplit`); each step reads its part with `atrim` (in samples, 100 ms further, since `atempo` gives a few milliseconds more or less), `asetpts`, its tempo (or `asetrate` when `keepPitch` is false), and is cut to its exact length (`apad`, `atrim`), every step but the last 5 ms longer. Neighbours are joined by `acrossfade` over those 5 ms with `hsin` (gains that add up to 1, as both sides hold nearly the same sound), one pair at a time (more inputs need FFmpeg 7.1), so the next step fades in from its own start: no jump at a boundary, each step stays on the picture and the segment keeps its length. All of this comes before the clip's own settings, envelopes and fades. Labels carry the segment's number and the step's.
  - `export.commands.ts`: `enqueue` checks everything and keeps the document as it is, so later edits don't reach a queued export.
  - `export.runner.ts`: one job at a time, outside the bus. A file with a proxy gets its master first (`media.prepareMaster`); a job with `matchTarget` is then measured (status `measuring`, by the first `loudnessMatchers`, cancellable) and its own spec gets the correction (`logic/correction.ts`'s `withCorrection`: `mix.master.correction`, a copy; the queued spec and the job's document are left as they are, `export.runner.test.ts`); the preview is asked for the webview's video levels (`preview.videoLevels()`, measured once per kernel; the renderer corrects video layers with them, and when they can't be measured the export goes on and says its colors may look a little flat), then each frame is drawn by `preview.createRenderer` and posted to FFmpeg, which sets the pace.
  - **Match the delivery target** (`export.enqueue`'s `matchTarget`, the form's `matchTarget`, a switch under When saving shown when a matcher is there, and `sound`'s Stems card's "Match target on export", the same value; off while the export has no sound): the mix in the file is brought to the composition's delivery target without changing the project. `MasterMixSpec.correction` (`MasterCorrection`: `gainDb` on top of the master's level, its line's points too, and a `limiter` after its effects when the master has none working) is the one override, used by the export and by `sound`'s Match target for its measuring on the side. Stems are taken before the master, so they never get it. `export.matchNeedsSound`, `export.cantMatch`. The figures it works from are measured before the codec: MP3, AAC and Opus can add a few tenths of a dB of true peak.
  - `export.mixspec.ts` (public `soundOnlySpec(ctx, compositionId)`): the `SoundOnlySpec` (`duration`, `sound`, `mix`) of a whole composition as a Mix export mixes it, through the same `mixdown`; `sound`'s whole-mix analysis measures it.
  - `logic/mixdown.ts`: what sounds in a stretch of a composition, at any depth, as stretches of the original files, with their speed or tempo steps, and how the tracks they sit on mix. It returns `{ segments, mix }`. Muted tracks and disabled clips are left out; clips under a junction get equal-power fades.
  - The mix tree: each `SoundSegment` has `track`, an index into `ExportSpec.mix.tracks` (the composition's audio tracks in order, each `{ gain, pan }` as a linear level) beside `mix.master` (`MasterMixSpec`: `gain`, 0 when the master is muted, its input trim `trimDb` added to the fader on the same level stage; `gainEnvelope` instead when it has a volume line, built as a track's by `trackLevel`; `pan`, missing when centred; `effects`). A clip sounds through its top-level track; inside a placed composition it takes the outer track's index and the inner tracks' volume and pan are folded into its own gain and pan by `soundInPlacement` (`project`), the function the preview's `placeSound` uses, so both sides fold alike. Solo is never here: it isn't in the document. `src-tauri/src/export/mixgraph.rs` builds the tree as the preview's buses: the clips of a track are summed (`amix`), the track's `volume` and `soundfilters::pan_filter` apply to that sum (a track with a volume line has `TrackMixSpec.gainEnvelope` instead of `gain`, below), the tracks are summed, the master's `volume` (or its line's `gain_envelope_filter`) and `pan_filter` (a balance with a track pan's law) follow, then its effects, then `apad,atrim` to `[mixed]`. Stems are split off before the master, so its pan, mute and line change only the mix. A track at the defaults adds no bus and no filter (its clips go straight to the last sum, which equals summing them first), so a default mix is one `amix` of every clip, as before. **Groups:** `mix.buses` (`BusMixSpec`: `gain`, `muted`, `effects`) are the composition's groups in order, and a track sending to one has `TrackMixSpec.bus`, its index (a group that isn't there sends to the master). `mixgraph.rs` builds a group with the same `Bus` as a track (the outputs of its tracks summed → its effects → its `volume`, no pan, no line), between them and the master `amix`; tracks and groups are nodes of one tree (`Node`), built in an order where a group follows its tracks and every bus follows what its duck listens to. A track's volume line stays on the track, before the group. A muted group mutes its tracks, as their own mute would: `mixdown` leaves their clips out, so they sound nowhere and key no duck, and the group adds no input (`mixgraph.rs` drops any segment of such a track with its chain, should one come). Inside a placed composition, an inner group's volume and mute fold into its tracks' clips through `soundInPlacement` and `trackBus` (`project`), as the preview does; its effects aren't heard from outside. Checked on 9 Nov 2026 (FFmpeg n9.0.2): three tones, two in a group at 0.5 with one track at 0.5, rendered from the graph `mixgraph.rs` writes, equal the preview's gains (`c + 0.5 × (0.5a + b)`) to the last float bit; with the group muted, only the direct track.
  - Effects in the mix: `TrackMixSpec.effects` and `mix.master.effects` carry the chains that change the sound as `MixEffect` (`export.types.ts`): off effects, noise reduction (a rendered file, below) and an EQ without a working band are left out, and a duck's `listensTo` becomes indexes into `mix.tracks` and, for groups, `listensToBuses`, indexes into `mix.buses` (a track or group not there is dropped; a muted one keeps its index but has no clips, so it keys nothing). A duck may sit on a group. Effects of the tracks inside a placed composition are not applied, as in the preview. `src-tauri/src/export/mixeffects.rs` turns each into the filter the preview's ports match sample for sample (the table in `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-03-sound-dynamics-check.md`): `acompressor`/`agate`/`sidechaincompress` with `detection=peak`, `deesser=m=0.5:f=√(1−e^(−2π·Hz/22050))`, `alimiter=attack=5:level=disabled` (latency off) between a `pan` to eight channels and `aiir` (each channel delayed, and the true-peak interpolator's three phases, in doubles) before it and a `pan` back to the delayed two after, so it limits true peaks as the preview's does, the duck at `threshold=-40dB:ratio=20:mix=1−10^(depth/20)`; the EQ effect reuses `soundfilters::eq_filters`, the clip EQ's. The order is the preview bus's: a track is its clips summed → its effects in order → pan → level; the master is the tracks summed → its level → its effects → `apad,atrim`. A duck's key is the tracks it listens to after their levels: each listened track ends in `asplit` (`[t<n>]` for the master, `[k<n>_<listener>]` for each duck), the key is their `amix` (`normalize=0`), and the ducked track goes through `sidechaincompress`. Tracks are built listened-first (a loop, which the document refuses, drops the key of its first track). A duck whose tracks have no sound in the range is left out (a silent key lowers nothing), and a track with no sound keys or splits nothing. A chain with effects, and the key, are padded in silence to the export's length plus 100 ms, so the limiter gives back its lookahead and the duck never ends waiting for its key, as the preview's effects run on; the last `atrim` cuts it. A duck on the master is ignored (refused by the document). Checked end to end against the preview's runner: see the check doc.
  - Noise reduction: a clip heard through it (`project`'s `heardDenoise`, the rule `denoiseNeeds` and the preview follow: its top-level track's, otherwise the master's; inside a placement, the outer track's; a reversed clip's rendered file gets `areverse` like any reversed sound) is mixed from its file rendered with those settings (`media.denoised`, the forward rendering, which `sound`'s follower always wants too), so the segment's `path` is that FLAC and `mixeffects.rs` has no filter for it. `mixdown` takes the file through `soundFile`; `export.guards.ts` (`denoisedFiles`, `checkDenoised`) asks for each, which starts a rendering that hadn't, and `export.enqueue` fails with `export.denoise_pending` while any is still rendering.
  - A track's volume line (`TrackMix.volumeLine`, keys on timeline time): `mixdown`'s `mix.tracks[i].gainEnvelope` is `envelope(t => dbToLinear(trackValue(line, t) + volumeDb), start, end, keyMoments(line, start, end))` over the export range, `at` counted from the range's start (so an export that starts after 0 starts the line there), `gain` 1; the fader's volume is the offset on top. `mixgraph.rs` applies it with `soundfilters::gain_envelope_filter` where the fader's `volume` goes (after the effects and pan, before a duck's `asplit`, so a duck's key hears it, as in the preview), its `t` counting from the export's start. A long line (an hour, a point a second) is just a longer expression: the graph goes to the graph file below. Inside a placed composition an inner track's line is folded into its clips, as its volume is: `walk` carries the lines of the inner tracks above as a `Swell` (`MixNesting.swell`, `logic/swell.ts` `withLine`: a factor on the export's clock and the times its keys fall on), and every segment under one gets a `gainEnvelope` sampled by `envelope` with those keys as breaks (with the clip's own keyed volume, when it has some), as the preview plays them on the element's chain.
  - Keyed volume and pan: `logic/envelope.ts` samples a value at each key's timeline time (`project`'s `keyMoments`: loop and ping-pong repeats included, and 1 ms before each, so a Hold step stays a step) and every 100 ms, with no cap, and drops the points on the straight line between their neighbours (`project`'s `simplify(points, tolerance)`, which `sound` uses at 0.5 dB for drawn strokes and fader passes) as `gainEnvelope` / `panEnvelope`, `at` counted from the segment's start, read with `trackValue(track, contentTime(clip, time))`. They replace `gain` and `pan`; the parent placement's gain and a crossfade still multiply in. `soundfilters.rs` turns them into expressions of `t` applied by `aeval`, sample by sample (`envelope_gain`, used by `gain_envelope_filter` and `pan_envelope_filter`; a constant is a plain `volume`): gain where the gain goes, and a pan through four gains (`pan_gains`, the law of the still `pan_filter`) between `channelsplit` and `join`, before the fades. Expressions are balanced trees of `if` (FFmpeg stops parsing at 100 levels of nesting), constant stretches collapse. Not `volume=eval=frame`: it reads `t` once per decoded frame (1024 to 4096 samples), so a steep fade moved in steps. Measured on 9 Nov 2026 (FFmpeg n9.0.2, a tone through 0 → −40 dB over 200 ms, points every 100 ms, against the preview's straight line in gain): largest step and deviation 17.95 dB from WAV, 8.67 dB from AAC, 7.43 dB from MP3; splitting frames first (`asetnsamples`) still left 3.4 / 1.9 / 1.0 dB at 256 / 128 / 64 samples; `aeval` follows the line exactly (largest step 0.076 dB, the line's own slope per sample). It costs about 0.85 s of CPU per envelope per minute of stereo sound at 600 points (1 minute, four clips and a track line with 600 points each: 1.1 s → 4.6 s audio-only, no difference measurable beside a 1080p30 x264 encode, ~11 s either way). `recipe.rs` puts the segments' chains together with `mixgraph.rs`'s tree. A graph over 8 KB goes in `<partial>.filter` (Windows allows about 32 KB for a whole command line) and is read with `-/filter_complex` (FFmpeg 7+) or `-filter_complex_script` (older; `ffmpeg -version` decides, asked once off the async runtime's thread and kept for the app's lifetime: `job::script_option`); the file is removed on success, failure, cancel and by the start-up sweep.
  - `logic/timing.ts`: a frame is drawn 1 ms into its time. A time rounded to microseconds can fall just before its frame, and a seek there lands on the previous one.
  - Audio formats: besides MP3, a sound-only export can be WAV (`pcm_s16le` or `pcm_s24le`), FLAC (`-sample_fmt s16` or `s32` with `-bits_per_raw_sample 24`), AAC (in `.m4a`) or Opus (`libopus`, `.opus`). `ExportSpec.sampleRate` (44100 or 48000) and `bitDepth` (16 or 24, WAV and FLAC only) are part of the spec, set by the form (`ExportFormState.sampleRate` and `bitDepth`, view state, shown in the Settings section and in `sound`'s Audio format card) and by `export_enqueue`'s `sampleRate` and `bitDepth`. `spec.rs` (`ExportSpec::check`, run by `export_begin`) refuses any other rate or depth, or a depth on another format; Opus is always written at 48000, and so is any format with a picture. A 16-bit WAV or FLAC is dithered (triangular) where the mix is cut down to 16 bits: `recipe.rs` adds an `aresample=<rate>:osf=s16:dither_method=triangular` step to the graph for the mix and each stem (a stream fed from a graph takes no `-af`). The mix itself is always made at 48000 (`SAMPLE_RATE`, the working rate every filter counts samples in); `-ar` resamples it to the file's rate on the way out. Checked on 9 Nov 2026 (FFmpeg n9.0.2, `ffprobe`): WAV at 44.1 kHz 24 bit is `pcm_s24le`, 44100 Hz, 24 bits; FLAC 44.1 kHz 24 bit, 24 bits; Opus 48000 Hz; AAC 44100 Hz.
  - **Stems** (`export.enqueue`'s `stems`, the form's `stems`, a switch under What to export, and `sound`'s Stems card, which sets the same value): a sound-only export can also write each group, and Other, as files of their own, in the same FFmpeg run, one job of the queue. `logic/stems.ts` (`stemsOf`, in `index.ts`) says which: one per group of the composition in order (at any values; a muted or empty group's file is silence of the full length), then Other when some audio track sends straight to Master (or to a group that isn't there). Files are `<name> - <group>.<ext>`, `<name> - Other.<ext>` and `<name> - Mix.<ext>` (the mix, the job's `path`; the stems' are `ExportJob.stems`); a group's name is made safe on Linux and Windows (`/\:*?"<>|` and control characters become `_`, no dots or spaces at the end) and unique beside Mix, Other and the other groups, ignoring case (`Music 2`). `export.guards.ts`'s `outputPaths` numbers the files together (`<name> (2) - Mix.wav` beside `<name> (2) - Dialogue.wav`) and keeps clear of the queue's stems too. A stem is taken after its group (its effects and level) and before Master: Master's level and effects are only in the mix. Solo is never in the export. With no group there is nothing to split (Other would only repeat the mix): the Export page's switch and `sound`'s Stems card are disabled with "Add a group to export stems." and `export.enqueue` refuses `stems` with `export.noGroups` (`logic/stems.ts`'s `hasGroups`). The stems the form lists come from `ui/export.composition.hook.ts`'s `useExportComposition` (the composition the page exports, the compositions to choose from, its stems and whether it has groups), which `sound`'s Stems card reads too, so both show the same. `logic/stems.ts`'s `stemSpecs` pairs each stem with its path for the spec. `ExportSpec.stems` (`StemSpec`: `output`, `bus`, none for Other) reaches `spec.rs`, which refuses stems of a format with a picture, of no sound, of a group that isn't there, or twice the same (`ExportSpec::check`); `mixgraph.rs`'s `stem_taps` sums what each stem sends to the master (a group's output, even a default group's, whose tracks otherwise go straight to the master `amix`; Other's, the direct tracks' outputs) and splits it (`asplit=2`): one copy into the master `amix`, the other padded and cut like the mix to `[stem<i>]`; a stem with nothing sounding is `anullsrc` cut to the length. `arguments` adds one output per stem after the mix's: `-map [stem<i>] -ar <rate>`, the mix's encoder settings, `-t`, its partial file. `job.rs` writes `<file>.partial.<ext>` for each, renames them all when FFmpeg succeeds (all or none: a file already there is set aside and put back if a later rename fails) and removes them all on failure or cancel; the start-up note lists them, one a line. Checked on 9 Nov 2026 (FFmpeg n9.0.2, WAV 24 bit): four tones on two groups (one with a compressor at 0.5) and a direct track: the three stems summed equal the mix within 1.2e-7 (0.00000 dB, residual −119 dB); with Master at 0.5 and a limiter at −12 dB, the stems were bit for bit the same and their sum equals the mix before Master (0.00000 dB), the mix itself 6 dB down; with a group muted its stem is silent, 3 s long, and the rest still add up to the mix.
  - `logic/presets.ts`: kinds, formats, rates, depths and the 10–100 quality scale as encoder numbers. `logic/estimate.ts`: rough size and time.
  - `export.guards.ts`: what `enqueue` refuses, and where the file goes.
  - `export.service.ts`: the native calls. `ui/`: the page.
  - Native (`src-tauri/src/export`): `spec.rs` holds the spec types (`ExportSpec`, `StemSpec`, the mix specs, `MasterCorrection`) and `check()` (a correction: a number within ±72 dB, its limiter's ceiling -20..0 dB and release over 0, and no limiter added to a master that has one; checked for an analysis too); `MasterMixSpec::corrected` folds the correction in, which `mixgraph.rs` applies after the stems are split off; `recipe.rs` turns a spec into FFmpeg arguments and builds each segment's chain from `soundfilters.rs` (tempo, pitch, equalizer, pan, envelopes), `mixgraph.rs` joins the chains in the track and master tree, `job.rs` runs one FFmpeg on `<name>.partial.<ext>` (and its stems') and renames them when complete (it also runs `sound`'s whole-mix analysis: `Job::measure` over `recipe.rs`'s `measure_arguments`, the same mix graph from a `SoundOnlySpec` with no stems, `[mixed]` to `-f null`, every stderr line to a listener, finished with `measured`, cancelled as an export is, and noted in the same folder so a crash leaves no filter-graph file), `frames.rs` takes frames as raw RGBA over loopback HTTP (a frame is too big for the JSON path IPC takes on Linux), `encoders.rs` says what this FFmpeg can write and which graphics card encoder really works. Quitting stops every FFmpeg: on `RunEvent::Exit`, `lib.rs` calls `export::stop` (every export job cancelled, partial files removed), `sound::stop` (every measuring job, through `Measures::cancel_all`) and `media::stop` (proxy, master, derived-file and image encoders, which watch `media/stopper.rs`, kill their FFmpeg and remove their `*.partial.*` files, a reversed copy's work folder too, and the same sweep runs at startup). A crash can't run that, so the partial file left by one is removed on the next launch.
- **Facts worth knowing:** about 13 frames a second at 1080p on the development machine. Size and frame rate come from the composition. FFmpeg is the system binary; without it the page says how to install it.
- **Not yet:** VAAPI, pausing a job, exporting several compositions in one action.
