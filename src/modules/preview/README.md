# preview

Playback and the preview: the playhead, the engine that draws the timeline at the playhead, the
viewer (zoom and pan around the frame) and the preview panel with its transport bar.

- **Tier:** core
- **Requires:** `project`, `element` (how each kind of clip is drawn), `junction` (how the two
  clips of a junction are mixed), `media`, `fonts`
- **Speed:** an element plays at its clip's rate (`syncSlot` sets it each time, since loading a file resets it, and a released slot goes back to 1), and drift limits scale with the rate. A ramped clip plays at `rateAt(clip, time)` (`project`), set only when it moved by more than 0.01, its element is placed by `sourceTimeAt`, drift limits follow that rate, and a speed drag (`previewProps`) doesn't reach it. A clip without pitch correction gets the shift through the pitch worklet (`createSoundAtSpeed`), following the rate in 0.01 steps (each rate keeps its own sound object). A reversed clip plays `reversedUrl`; until that copy is ready the clip is left out of the frame and `whenReversed` asks for a redraw. A held frame stays paused.
- **Keys:** `resolveFrame` puts `animatedClip(clip, time)` (`project`) in each active clip, and `animatedClip(clip, clip.start)` in each upcoming one, so the engine draws keyed values with no other change. A placement's inner frame is still found from the saved clip.
- **Disabled clips** are left out of `resolveFrame`; a junction beside one isn't active.
- **Contributes:** panel `preview.panel` (main); overlay `preview.fieldGuide` (`previewOverlays`: the field guide and corner marks, DOM only, so never in a capture or export); keybindings `preview.toggle-play` (Space),
  `preview.seek-start` (Home). Playback pauses when another page covers the editor
- **Points it owns:** `previewOverlays` (`preview.points.ts`): layers drawn over the frame, sized
  and moved with it, positioned in % of the frame (`transform` contributes its selection box). Each overlay's
  wrapper ignores the pointer (`pointer-events-none`); an overlay turns it back on for the parts
  that take input, so overlays stacked later never block the ones below
- **API** (`PreviewApi`, per kernel): `playback` (the user's clock), `clockFor(actor)` / `dropClock` (each collaborator has a `logic/virtual.clock.ts` playhead: wall-clock time read on demand, no frames, nothing drawn; commands use `clockFor(ctx.actor)` so an agent never moves the user), `attachCanvas` (canvases stack: the kernel's one engine is made on the first canvas and draws on the newest attached only, through `PreviewEngine.setCanvas`; when that one detaches, the one under it is drawn on again, so the Sound page's small stage and the editor's never draw at once. The engine, its media elements and its sound graph stay across the switch, so playback has no gap and the loudness keeps counting; the engine goes only when no canvas is left), `redraw`,
  `captureAt(time)` (`preview.capture.ts`: its own picture-only engine on a detached canvas, polled with timers because frames stop on a hidden window, disposed after 30 s idle; never touches the user's preview; an undecoded frame is an error, not a stale picture; checked on WebKitGTK with the window on another workspace, 45 s hidden, 16 of 16 exact frames; `document.hidden` stayed false there, so nothing may depend on visibility events; not checked on WebView2), `createRenderer({ project, compositionId, mediaUrl? })` (`preview.capture.ts`: a hidden picture-only engine at the composition's full size; `frameAt(time)` waits up to 30 s for every layer and returns RGBA bytes read with `getImageData`, which is what export sends to FFmpeg; the canvas keeps the same 2D context, never `willReadFrequently`; `project` is a snapshot, so later edits don't reach it), `measureVideoLevels(url)` (`preview.levels.ts`: what the canvas holds for video black and white; WebKitGTK squeezes the range, black at 15 and white at 236, while other layers are right, so `createRenderer`'s `videoLevels` corrects video layers only, through a lookup table in `engine.levels.ts`; the live preview doesn't, because reading every frame back is too slow to play), `previewTransform(clipId, transform | null)` (draws a live transform during a
  canvas gesture without changing the project), `previewProps(clipId, patch | null)`
  (a live patch of any element's values drawn over the saved ones until cleared; groups may be
  partial; a `transform` patch moves any picture, media included), `onPropsOverride(listener)` (fires on set/clear) and `propsOverride(clipId)` (so the
  canvas tools measure the picture being drawn), `viewport` (`store`, `zoomBy`, `panBy`, `fit`),
  `sound` (`PreviewSound`, `preview.sound.ts`, ephemeral, never in the document or an export: `previewMix(trackId | "master", patch | null)` plays a live `Partial<TrackMix>` over the saved mix until cleared, for a fader or pan drag that commits one `sound.*` command on release; `setSolo(trackIds)` (none means all sound); `setMonitor({ mono, dim, speakerDb })` (`DEFAULT_MONITOR` is stereo, not dimmed, 0 dB); `levels()` gives `MixLevels`, the instantaneous peak of each track bus and the master in dBFS (`-Infinity` for silence), with no peak hold, for a caller to read per frame; `loudness()` gives the `LoudnessReading` of the master, measured only while playing, since the last `resetLoudness()` (paused, the last reading stands) (momentary and short-term LUFS, gated integrated LUFS, range in LU, true peak in dBTP, the maximum since the last reset, which never falls back), `-Infinity` where a window hasn't filled, and null until the worklet has posted its first reading, or for good if it couldn't load). Each change makes a new `LiveSound` handed to the engine at once (`PreviewEngine.setSound`), and to a newly attached engine
- **Files:**
  - `preview.clock.ts`: the playhead, driven by `requestAnimationFrame`; listeners are called per
    frame without React.
  - `engine/`: the v1 `PreviewEngine`. `engine.html.ts` renders and draws video layers bottom → top,
    each placed with `clipPlacement` (`project`); `engine.slots.ts` pools media elements (reused
    across clips of the same file, so splits play seamlessly; clips starting within 1.5 s are
    preloaded); `engine.sync.ts` keeps elements on the clock and re-seeks only on drift
    (`logic/drift.ts`). The clock moves during a seek and a seek flushes the sound (an audible
    tick), so a seek aims where the clock will be when it lands, using what the slot's last seek
    took (`seekCost`). A seek to the clock itself lands late on long-GOP files, stays over the
    limit and repeats every 500 ms.
    Elements are chosen by track kind: clips on video tracks get a `<video>` silenced with
    `volume = 0` (pictures only; WebKitGTK stops handing a `muted` video's frames to the canvas);
    clips on audio tracks get an `<audio>`, also for a video file's sound (same URL, sound only).
    Images never take a media element: `engine.images.ts` (`ImageCache`) keeps decoded stills and
    frames of animated images by URL, up to a pixel budget, and rasterizes SVGs once. `imageFrames`
    (`media`) names the picture at the clip's own time, looped, and the second ahead is loaded early, so a stretched GIF
    keeps moving; scrubbing moves it too. The engine waits for the picture like any other layer, and a broken one never blocks.
    Each clip is drawn as its element's `picture` says: `source: "media"` through a media element
    as above; any other element (text, a plugin's) with its own `draw(ctx, props, env)`, at
    `clipPlacement(format, size(props), transform, fit)`, the same box the canvas tools use. Props
    are the clip's resolved values with the live override merged over them. The transform is the
    gesture's (`previewTransform`) if any, else `clipTransform(clip, override.transform)`. `env.time` is the time since the clip started. A `picture.ready` that says no holds the frame like an undecoded video layer (`logic/readiness.ts`), and clips about to start are asked ahead so their art is loaded in time. `env.ensureFont` is
    `fonts.ensure`; the module redraws on `fonts.onLoaded`; `env.measure` is `canvasTextMeasure`
    (`element`) over the engine's canvas. An element without `picture` is not drawn. Swap engines by
    implementing `PreviewEngine` (`preview.types.ts`).
    **Composite:** a clip at Normal and 100% draws straight onto its target. Any other is painted on `MixCanvases.layer()` and lands once through `landLayer` (`engine.composite.ts`), with `canvasBlend` (`logic/blend.ts`) as the canvas operation, because text paints in several passes that would otherwise blend one by one. Export and captures use the same path. Under a junction a picture has no backdrop, so each clip keeps only its opacity: when both share a mode, the mix draws on `MixCanvases.result()` and that lands with the mode; when they differ it lands Normal for the overlap. Inside a placement a clip blends with what's in that composition; the placement's own mode blends with what's under it.
    **Junctions (the mixing hook):** during a junction's overlap a video track has two active
    clips. `engine.mix.ts` (`drawTrack`) draws each with the normal placement path into one of
    two pooled full-frame offscreen canvases (`MixCanvases`: default accelerated 2D canvases,
    never `willReadFrequently`), then calls the kind's `mix(ctx, a, b, progress, props, env)` on
    the main canvas in that track's place in the bottom → top order, inside `save`/`restore` in a `try/finally`: a mix that throws is logged
    once per engine and that frame shows the right picture over the left. `env` has the format,
    `kx`/`ky`, `elapsed` (seconds since the overlap started) and `random()`, a generator seeded by
    the junction id and re-created each frame (`logic/random.ts`), so noise is the same on every
    frame. A kind without `mix` (or a kind that isn't registered) draws nothing extra: the clips
    draw in start order, so the right covers the left. Nothing here is transition-specific;
    effects can reuse the hook later. The frame is painted only when both clips' frames are ready
    (rule 22). Each clip id has its own element (the pool only reuses a _free_ one), so the two
    halves of a split joined by a junction play at once; the right clip is preloaded from
    `start - 1.5 s` like any upcoming clip, and waiting pictures are silenced with volume 0, not
    `muted`, so their frame is drawable when the overlap starts. A split with no junction still
    reuses the warm element. `engine.audio.ts` sets each element's sound: linked sound clips
    overlapping under a junction get an equal-power crossfade (`soundGains`, `crossfadeGains` in
    `logic/junctions.ts`), the left clip at its end and the right at its start, which `fadeLevel`
    (`project`) folds into the clip's own fades as the export does.
    **Sound: one graph.** Every element of an audio track goes through Web Audio (`engine.audiograph.ts`; an element's own chain and the pitch worklet are in `engine.soundchain.ts`), so every sound is metered:
    `element → clip chain (pitch, EQ, pan, level) → track bus (upmix, effects, pan, fader, solo, meter) → master (fader, effects, meter) → monitor (mono, dim, speaker) → destination`.
    The AudioContext and its buses are made when the first sound element is attached, never after the graph is disposed; `resume()` is never awaited (`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-02-audio-preview-check.md`). What the mix was checked against (export loudness, pan, defaults against the old graph, and the hand checks still open): `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-03-sound-mix-check.md`. If they can't be made, the user is told once per engine and it isn't tried again: every element plays plain. An element takes one `MediaElementSource`, ever: once attached it stays attached and plays at volume 1. One that fails plays plain at its own level, outside the mix, and the user is told once per element with `notify("error", …)` naming the file. `busOf` never makes a context: before one exists it gives nothing. The pool hands an element to clips of any track, so `voiceSlot` calls `chain.routeTo(graph.busOf(trackId))` each frame (a no-op unless the bus changed). A clip sounds through the bus of its top-level track; a clip inside a placed composition goes through the bus of the track the placement sits on, with each inner track's volume and pan applied in its own chain through `soundInPlacement` (`project`; the export folds the same way), the pans of inner tracks summed (`placeSound` in `logic/mix.ts`). Inner tracks' `muted` silences the element as before; inner effects, groups and master aren't heard from outside.
    `engine.soundnodes.ts` holds the parts chains and buses share (`createUpmix`, `createEqFilters`, `setBand`, `createPanner`, and `glide`, which moves a parameter over 10 ms without a click). `engine.mixbus.ts` (`createMixBus`) makes a bus per audio track of the open composition, starting at its pan and level so a muted or soloed-out track never blips, and drops it when the track goes; the master meter sits after the master fader, before the monitor. Mono sums both sides into each speaker through a `ChannelMergerNode` at half each, Dim is −20 dB. A track's `muted` sets the bus fader to 0 and solo sets a gain of its own after it to 0, never the elements, so sync isn't disturbed. Solo outlives a deleted track and a switch of composition, so `mixUpdateOf` keeps only the solo ids that are tracks of the open composition; none left means no solo. `engine.meters.ts` (`createMeter`) splits a bus to one `AnalyserNode` per side and reads the largest sample of the last 2048. `engine.soundmix.ts` (`createSoundMix`) is the engine's sound side: the graph, the `LiveSound` from the controller (`setSound`), `levels()`, `heard` (`createSoundAtSpeed`), and `update(project, composition)`, called at each frame, which calls `graph.mix(mixUpdateOf(...))` (`logic/mix.ts`) only when the project's tracks, the open composition or the `LiveSound` changed, never per frame; live values win over the document's until cleared. A picture-only engine has no graph.

    **Effects.** A bus plays its track's `effects` (`SoundEffect`, from `project`) in the user's order: `input → [effects] → pan → fader → solo → meter`; the master's run after its fader and before its meter, the loudness tap and the monitor, so a limiter there is the last thing before them. `engine.effects.ts` (`createEffectChain(context, from, to, dynamics)`) makes a chain between two nodes it doesn't own. `eq` is the clip's six biquads (`createEqFilters`/`setBand`); every other live kind is one `AudioWorkletNode` running `dynamics.worklet.ts`, a thin wrapper over `createEffectRunner` (`logic/effects.ts`), which runs any kind by `kind` on stereo blocks over the pure ports in `logic/` (one detector for both sides). Noise reduction plays a rendered file instead, so it has no node. A chain is rebuilt only when its kinds, order or ids change, or the worklet becomes ready or fails; any other change posts the effect to its node (`DynamicsCommand`, when the effect object changed) or sets the biquads, and the processor keeps its state, so a drag doesn't click. Off, an effect passes the sound through and its state stands still; turned back on it starts from rest (envelope, de-esser, the limiter's lookahead and schedule), so no old sound replays. A duck's node has a second input: the sum (a stereo `GainNode`) of the tracks it listens to after their faders and before their solo gain, so the key follows their level and mute but not solo, which is only how the user listens and which the export doesn't have; `mix` makes and levels every bus first and sets chains after, so the listened buses exist, and rewires the key when `listensTo` changes or a listened bus comes or goes. The tracks inside a placed composition keep their effects to themselves: from outside only their volume and pan are heard. `engine.dynamics.ts` (`createDynamicsNodes`) loads the worklet once per context, in the background; until it's ready, dynamics effects pass dry. If it can't load, a node can't be made or a processor fails later (`onprocessorerror`), the user is told once per engine, the chains rebuild without worklet nodes and play dry from then on; playback never breaks. The limiter's 5 ms lookahead delays its bus by 5 ms, as `alimiter` does in the export.

    **Loudness.** `engine.loudness.ts` (`createLoudnessTap`) loads `loudness.worklet.ts` (bundled as a URL with `?worker&url`, the way `engine.soundchain.ts` loads the pitch processor) and connects the master, after its fader, to it, beside the monitor (Mono, Dim and the speaker don't reach it); the node also goes to the destination so the context keeps running it, but it writes no output. The worklet is a thin wrapper over the pure `createLoudnessMeter` (`logic/loudness.ts`: K-weighting derived for any sample rate in `kweight.ts`, 400 ms blocks every 100 ms, gating in `gating.ts` as histograms of a hundredth of an LU so memory doesn't grow however long it plays, true peak by 4x oversampling in `truepeak.ts`, a Hann-windowed sinc that approximates BS.1770-4 Annex 2's filter rather than using its tabulated coefficients: it reads between about −0.3 and +0.1 dB of a steady sine's true peak, inside Tech 3341's +0.2 / −0.4 dB) and posts a reading every 100 ms; the main thread keeps the latest. It works only while active: the engine calls `SoundMix.setPlaying` at each `render` (with `playing`) and on `pauseAll`, the tap posts `{ active }` to the worklet only when it changes (`LoudnessCommand`), and a tap that loads later starts from the graph's last state. Inactive, the worklet neither measures nor posts, so paused time doesn't count and the last reading stands (momentary and short-term don't fall to −∞ on a pause). `resetLoudness()` posts `{ reset: n }`, and readings from before it (older `generation`) are ignored. The graph opens it once per engine: if the worklet can't load, or its processor fails later (`onprocessorerror`), the user is told once and loudness reads null from then on while everything else goes on. The master's gain is set outright by the first mix; later changes glide.

  - `logic/`: `junctions.ts` (`activeJunctions(project, trackId, time)`: overlapping junctions
    under the playhead with their `progress` and `elapsed`; `crossfadeGains(progress)`;
    `soundGains(project, composition, time)`), `random.ts` (`seededRandom(seed)`), `drift.ts`
    (`driftSeek(state)`: where to seek a playing element, or `null`), `db.ts` (`peakToDb(peak)`:
    a linear peak in dBFS, `-Infinity` for silence, shared by the meters and the true peak),
    `dynamics.ts` (the one dynamics core, a port of FFmpeg's detector and gain curve: `compressorCurve(params, sampleRate)` (`acompressor`), `gateCurve(gate, sampleRate)` (`agate`, downward expansion, its default knee and `GATE_RANGE`) and `duckCurve(duck, sampleRate)` (`sidechaincompress` at `DUCK_THRESHOLD_DB`, `DUCK_RATIO`, with `mix = 1 − 10^(depthDb/20)` so the track goes down by `depthDb` at most) each work out a `DynamicsCurve` once per settings change; `dynamicsGain(state, curve, level)` moves the detector (`createDynamicsState()`) by one level and gives the factor for the whole frame, so stereo is linked as FFmpeg's `link=average` (`linkedLevel(left, right)`); `dynamicsStep(state, curve, input)` is one mono sample; pure and allocation-free for a worklet), `effects.ts` (`createEffectRunner(sampleRate)`: one dynamics effect of any kind on stereo blocks, what the worklet runs), `deesser.ts` (`deesserStep(state, settings, sample)`: FFmpeg's `deesser`, one channel; `deesserFrequency(hz)` is its `f`) and `limiter.ts` (`limiterStep(state, settings, frame)`: FFmpeg's `alimiter` with a 5 ms lookahead, in place on an interleaved frame; the output is 5 ms late and never over the ceiling). Every one matches ffmpeg n9.0.2 to float precision; the check, and the exact filter each effect must be exported as (`detection=peak`, the limiter's `level=disabled`, the de-esser's `f`, the duck's `mix`), are in
    `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-03-sound-dynamics-check.md`.
  - `logic/frame.ts`: `resolveFrame(project, compositionId, time, options)`: a composition at a time as a tree, descending into placements at their local time (`sourceIn + time - start`). Every clip has a `path` of placement ids (`outer/inner`), because one original can be on screen twice: slots, needed sets and canvases key on it, never the bare clip id. A placement on a video track shows only pictures, on an audio track only sound; a hidden track shows nothing inside.
  - `engine/engine.nested.ts`: `NestedCanvases`: one transparent canvas per placement on screen (capped at the preview's size), with its own `MixCanvases`, since a transition inside a placement must not share canvases with one around it. Inner clips draw as saved: live gestures only touch the open composition. The background colour only fills the top level. Above 12 elements in a frame, inner clips are left out and the user is told once.
  - `preview.controller.ts`: wires clock, engine, project and media (injected, so it runs in
    node). It draws the composition the user has open (`project`'s `view`); an agent's clock runs on the length of its own open composition, and `captureAt(time, compositionId)` draws any composition. `logic/playheads.ts`: each composition keeps its playhead, restored on a switch. Renders every frame while playing, on demand while paused.
  - `preview.viewport.ts`: viewer zoom/pan state (view only, not undoable).
  - `ui/`: `PreviewStage` (exported, with `PlayButton` and `Timecode`, for a page of its own; `bare`: no overlays, so no canvas tools, and fitted, ignoring and not changing the viewer's zoom and pan) (frame + overlays, placed imperatively by `stage.layout.hook.ts`;
    Ctrl/Cmd + wheel zooms at the pointer, wheel and middle-drag pan via
    `stage.navigation.hook.ts`), `Transport` (`Timecode`, `PlayButton`, `ViewerZoom`).
  - Native (`src-tauri/src/webview`, Linux): WebKitGTK 2.54 repainted only part of the canvas
    layer on screen (a strip at the right of the video stayed black or showed an old frame, and
    sometimes the whole picture stayed black while audio played) although the canvas pixels were
    right. The webview turns WebKit's damage tracking off (`PropagateDamagingInformation`,
    `UseDamagingInformationForCompositing`); `CLOUDY_CLIP_WEBKIT_DAMAGE=1` keeps it, to check
    whether a WebKit update fixed it. A CPU canvas (`willReadFrequently`) is not an option: it
    draws video frames black.
